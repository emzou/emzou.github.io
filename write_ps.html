what to write in a personal statement when you don't who you are or what you're doing

disclaimer

all of this is my own personal thoughts ~ i have marginally more than no experience in academia and applications and life. none of this is prescriptive. also may not be the best advice! its just what i did.

"its very honest, but not strategic"

When I was writing my personal statement, I'd only been sending bits and pieces to my friends and mentors -- I didn't send the whole thing to others until two weeks before the deadline. I quickly found out that there's a specific format that academics are used to with personal statements (at least in the social sciences!), and that, not only was there too much distracting/unnecessary content, I was missing crucial components in describing what kind of researcher I wanted to be. Maybe the way of thinking I'm delineating below is apparent to you, but it certainly wasn't for me, so I've tried to sum up everything I learned from other people and from this past year as a PhD student, in the hopes it might help someone other blisteringly earnest idiot like me.

heuristics learned from other people 

1. Do not try to write something beautiful and true. Write something strategic; personal statement is more like writing code.
2. The word count should be a constraint, not a goal. If you don’t have anything else to say, don’t say it.
3. Sentences are real estate; every sentence should tell the reader something about /you/ in particular — if you are talking about an impact, the point should be what your unique perspective/relation/history with said impact is. If you’re bringing forth an argument, you should be explaining why your argument is different. 
4. If you cannot tell from the content that a sentence could have only been written by you, consider removing it.

my own heuristic

“I need to describe a person that simply cannot be possibly turned away from this program. There might be more impressive applicants, but they can go somewhere else; you need to pick me over them because I must be in /your/ program.”

actual construction

step one

This will be the introduction paragraph, but also organize the narrative you tell about yourself. Do not worry too much about how nice the sentence sounds, this is a cognitive task. 

  1. My goal is to {what you do with degree} because I {care about /fascinated with/invested in} 
  {solving/understanding/mitigating} {ABC}. 
  
  2. {ABC} is important because {some impact}. 
  
  3. However, {current problem with ABC*}. 
  
  4. We can resolve this by {what you would do with your degree}. 
  
  5. In pursuing this degree, I can {benefits from education} 
  in order to {what you would do with your degree}.

step two

Write out everything you’ve done in the past few years — it does not have to be successful, important, or professional. Pick out things you can make into discrete events, and clearly lead to something you learned. It doesn’t matter if it didn’t directly cause this (or even if this event was actually related to you learning said thing). It just needs to illustrate why this current version of you is related to ABC; that it only makes sense for this version of you to be at the school. 

Genres of discrete ‘things I learned’ 
  1. I was exposed to…  
  2. I was wrong about… 
  3. I became curious about… 
  4. I noticed that… 
  5. I was unsatisfied by… 
  6. I started to think about thing in {new way}… 
  7. I started applying {thing learned} to… 
  8. I was touched by… 

  Avoid
  1. I was inspired by… (vague, tells us nothing, difficult to connect to real experience) 
  2. I became passionate about… (waste of words, the whole point is to explain how you ‘became’ passionate)
Find the verb that gets you to the tangible experience/event as quickly as possible; think of potential events as things with input/outputs.

step three

Pick 3; these serve as the critical points of your narrative — before moving forward, see if you can: 

      1. Concisely describe your prior and posterior state to the event. 
        If it’s kind of complicated and hard to put into words, consider using a different one. 
        This is important because the point of the statement is to show that you are uniquely capable/ suited to tackle {ABC}
          ... perhaps even the /only/ person who can tackle {ABC} because of your life experience 
          (which of course, is always unique to you). 
      2. Explain how this event helped you grow in terms of tangible accomplishments/methods — 
          illustrate the nature of your competence / what you are exposed to. 
        a. However, what’s most important is that the events serve the narrative you declared in the beginning. 
            It’s much better to describe an idea you encountered in class, that you did nothing about, but helped you develop in some way to reach your current relationship with ABC. 
        b. Do not put things just because they’re the most impressive thing you’ve done. 
          Very few people’s resumes alone (when they are applying to school) will impress the admissions committee;
            people who have done the most objectively prestigious things do not get into programs,
            people who are relevant and have proved that they can grow get into programs. 
      3. Very simply connect the thing you learned to your current relationship to ABC. 
        a. If it’s too much of a stretch, consider other options, 
        even if its really impressive or congenital to who /you/ are as a person.

step four

Once you’ve settled on your three, start outlining the actual content; don’t worry about turning into paragraph form / word repetition yet. This is all a cognitive exercise. 

      

        1. for n in range (1,3):
        2. I {care} about {fixing/working on/contributing to**} ABC because of {Event_n}. 
        3. Before this, {describe trait that changed after Event_n}
        4. During {Event_n}, I did {whatever the actual event was}
          a. Preferably tangible. But it’s okay if it’s just something you started thinking about! 
        5. This {caused me***} to {thing you started doing differently} 
        6. If applicable — additional ways you developed said thing during Event_n; what it particularly challenged for you 
        7. After {Event_n}, {relevant feature of your current relationship with ABC}

step five

This brings us to your current relationship with ABC. I don’t have an exact outline you can put what’s relevant to you here because the form of this part really depends on ABC + your knowledge of it. But to get there: 

          1. Write out all the (realistic) ways that this relationship in particular — not the general task of solving/improving ABC — could play out in the future,
           if the person was completely dedicated to solving/improving ABC. 
          2.  Looking at all these candidates, check to see how naturally the step of ‘getting degree/being in the program’ fits. 
          3. If it is not apparent in the method 
          (e.g, “In order to mitigate environmental racial injustice, I want to create programs that educate people about the history of their local environment”... 
          presumably, you have set up that you think this is a good way to mitigate environment injustice 
          because you volunteered at a library or wrote an essay in some class or etc…) 
          try to find some angle in which getting your degree helps you do that (e.g “We must better understand how federal funds are allocated on local levels in practice, not based only on how they should be allocated”) 
          4.  The ‘school’ part should be very simple because, presumably, you actually do have some understanding of ABC and how people can address ABC, just find something that would help us to understand in order to address it.

step six

How this school (not the degree generally) in particular will help you achieve that; you should be name dropping here 

        1. Faculty work, class, methods, or research area
        2.  Labs, research centers, policy centers, law centers/journals
        3. Organizations, clubs, events 
        4. Pedagogy of program (it’s interdisciplinary, its quantitative, its applied, its community-based...) 
        5. School resources (but not just that you want access to this resource, this resource helps you get to ABC by…) 
        6. (I want to use your telescope in particular because of ABC…)

good night and good luck!

You should have all the content you need now, the next step is writing style / smoothing transition / word efficiency. I got the results I got because other people were willing to spend time helping me, and I still can’t repay them. As such, I am always happy to help with personal statements (though the help may not be good)
